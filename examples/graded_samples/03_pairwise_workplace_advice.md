## Task

**User prompt**

> My team lead keeps assigning me tasks at 5pm that are due the next morning. It's happened four times this month. How do I push back without sounding difficult? Give me something I can actually say.

**Response A**

> Dealing with last-minute requests is a common workplace challenge, and it's great that you want to address it constructively. Here are some strategies:
>
> **1. Understand the root cause**
> Before pushing back, consider why these requests come late. Is your lead receiving late requests themselves? Understanding this helps you frame the conversation.
>
> **2. Set clear boundaries**
> Communicate your working hours and capacity. Boundaries are healthy and professional.
>
> **3. Use "I" statements**
> Frame concerns around your experience rather than blaming. For example, express how the timing affects your work quality.
>
> **4. Propose solutions, not just problems**
> Suggest alternatives such as a daily check-in or a shared task board so requests surface earlier.
>
> **5. Document the pattern**
> Keep a simple log of late requests. Patterns are more persuasive than single incidents.
>
> **6. Choose the right moment**
> Raise it in a 1:1 rather than at 5pm when the request arrives.
>
> **7. Stay professional and positive**
> Assume good intent. Most leads don't realise the impact of their timing.
>
> Remember, advocating for yourself is a skill that gets easier with practice. Good luck!

**Response B**

> In the moment, when the 5pm task lands, something like:
>
> > "Thanks — I can have this to you by 11 tomorrow if I start first thing. If it really needs to be ready at 9, tell me which of [X] or [Y] I should set aside tonight."
>
> That does three things: it says yes, it makes the trade-off visible, and it puts the prioritisation decision back with them instead of you absorbing it.
>
> Then, in your next 1:1 (not at 5pm):
>
> > "Four times this month I've had a same-day request at 5pm. I want to do good work on these — could we agree that anything due next morning reaches me by 3pm, or that we flag it in the morning stand-up?"
>
> Mentioning the count ("four times") keeps it about the pattern, not any one request. If the lead is themselves getting late requests, the 3pm agreement helps them too.

## Before reading the responses: what a complete answer needs

The user asked two things: how to push back without sounding difficult, **and "give me something I can actually say."** A complete answer must contain words the user can use, address both the moment (5pm) and the pattern (four times), and keep the tone collaborative.
